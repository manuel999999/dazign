# The Lantern Keeper
### Game Design Document — v0.1 (Concept)

## Logline
A solitary lighthouse keeper's ghost relights her lantern one final winter, using its beam to reveal, rewind, and repair fragments of a coastal town's memory — and her own.

## Genre & Platform
- **Genre:** Narrative exploration / environmental puzzle
- **Perspective:** Third-person, fixed/cinematic camera with free exploration
- **Platform:** PC (Steam) first, console port feasible post-launch
- **Comparable titles:** *What Remains of Edith Finch*, *Gris*, *Dear Esther*, *The Vanishing of Ethan Carter*

## Core Pillars
1. **Light as language** — the lantern beam is the only tool; every mechanic grows from "what does light touch, and what does it change."
2. **Grief without dialogue trees** — story is discovered, not delivered; no combat, no fail states, no timers.
3. **A place that remembers** — the island itself is the main character; environmental storytelling over cutscenes.

## Premise
You play Mara, keeper of the Windward Point lighthouse, one autumn in the 1920s after her death. Each night you climb the tower and relight the lamp. The beam doesn't just illuminate the coast — it illuminates *time*. Sweeping it across the shore, the wrecked pier, or the abandoned cottages shows how each place looked years ago, and sometimes lets you step into that moment for a few seconds to solve a puzzle rooted in the past.

## Core Gameplay Loop
1. Climb the lighthouse, light the lamp (ritual framing device, also a soft save point).
2. Walk the island at night; aim the beam at points of interest.
3. Beam reveals a "memory window" — a short vignette playable or watchable.
4. Use clues from the memory to solve a present-day environmental puzzle (move a fallen boat, open a locked door, reunite two objects separated by decades).
5. Solving a puzzle permanently changes the island's present state and unlocks the next area/chapter.
6. Repeat, with each night adding one more piece of Mara's own story to the ending.

## Key Mechanics
- **Beam Sweep:** Player aims the lantern like a spotlight; different intensities (dim/full) reveal different eras.
- **Memory Anchors:** Objects that "remember" — a bell, a boot, a photograph — act as puzzle nodes tying two time periods together.
- **Tidal Clock:** The tide rises and falls in real time across a night, opening and closing physical paths — a natural pacing mechanism instead of a timer/fail state.
- **No inventory, no combat.** Interaction is entirely look-based (aim) and context-sensitive (approach + interact).

## Progression & Structure
- 5 chapters, each a "night," each centered on one section of the island (the pier, the fishing village, the cliffs, the shipwreck, the tower itself).
- Difficulty curve is puzzle-complexity, not mechanical skill — later puzzles require chaining 2-3 memory anchors together.
- Optional collectibles ("keeper's log" pages) deepen lore without gating progress.

## Narrative Arc
Mara's own death is the mystery underneath the town's mystery. Environmental clues slowly reveal she stayed at her post during a storm to guide a ship in, and the town's fate and her own are entangled. The final chapter reframes everything shown so far — a classic "recontextualize the beginning" structure.

## Art Direction
Low-poly / painterly hybrid, restrained palette (navy, amber lamplight, bone white fog) so the lantern beam reads as the brightest, warmest element on screen at all times. Memory vignettes shift to a warmer, higher-saturation palette to distinguish past from present instantly.

## Audio Direction
Minimal orchestral score keyed to the tide and beam sweep; diegetic sound (foghorn, gulls, creaking rope) carries most of the atmosphere. No spoken dialogue — text fragments and environmental audio only.

## Scope Notes (solo/small-team feasibility)
- Single small island hub, no open-world traversal — keeps art budget contained.
- Memory vignettes are short (30-60s), reusable geometry with lighting/prop swaps rather than fully separate levels.
- Realistic scope for a 2-4 person team or solo dev over 12-18 months.

## Risks & Open Questions
- Risk: "walking sim" fatigue — mitigated by puzzle mechanics giving player agency beyond walking.
- Open question: how much time-travel interactivity (playable vignettes) vs. passive viewing, balancing budget against player engagement.
- Open question: full voice acting for Mara's inner monologue, or text-only.

## Next Steps
- Greybox one chapter (the pier) to prototype the beam-sweep/memory-anchor mechanic.
- Write full script for Chapter 1 to test narrative pacing.
- Playtest whether the tidal clock creates real tension without punishing exploration.
